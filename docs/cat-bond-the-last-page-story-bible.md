# CAT BOND — THE LAST PAGE
## Narrative bible · Canonical story draft · 2026-11-23

> **Story promise:** A warm, character-led creature-collecting RPG that slowly reveals a devastating misunderstanding. The player begins by helping strangers recover memories, then discovers that the person they most want to find is the one taking those memories away.

## 1. The dramatic question

The player wakes without a name in a world where memories are written into the living Catdex. Across five lands, cats are losing the memories that make them themselves. A backward bell calls the player by a name they cannot remember.

The surface question is **“Who is calling me?”** The emotional question is **“If someone loves you so much that they would erase everyone else to bring you back, is that love still a bond?”**

The story's answer: a bond is not ownership, and returning to someone cannot mean taking away the lives they built while you were gone.

## 2. Canon and rules

- **The player:** the former First Bellkeeper. They willingly gave up their name and memories and stayed inside the Star Bell to seal the catastrophe. They are not secretly evil, chosen royalty, or a reincarnated deity.
- **Muling (暮铃):** the player's closest companion. Muling saw the player disappear as the seal closed, but did not see the sacrifice. Grief hardened into the belief that the player chose to leave.
- **The Star Bell:** an ancient instrument that stores shared memories as resonant threads. It cannot reverse time or resurrect the dead. It can only replay, transfer, or bind memories.
- **The Hollow:** places where memories have been pulled out. It is not a separate monster species; it is the visible wound left in the world.
- **The Reversed Bell:** Muling's attempt to reconstruct the player by taking memories from other cats. It can create an echo, never restore a person exactly as they were.
- **The six melodies:** five regional memories plus the hidden final page. They are both evidence of the past and components of Muling's ritual. Each recovery helps the player understand the truth, but also advances the ritual. This double meaning must be foreshadowed, not explained in one exposition dump.
- **The ending:** the player does not promise never to leave again. Muling must accept that love cannot guarantee permanence. The two choose a new relationship, not a rewind to childhood.

## 3. Main cast

### The player — The Unnamed Bellkeeper
Quietly observant, compassionate but not saintly. The player is allowed to doubt, become angry, refuse easy forgiveness, and ask hard questions. Their old sacrifice was a choice, but that does not mean the cost was fair to everyone left behind.

### Muling — The one who waited
Once curious, mischievous, and afraid of being left behind. In the present, Muling is controlled and unnervingly gentle; they rarely threaten the player directly. They speak as if the reunion is already decided. Their antagonist logic is understandable but never excused: “If the world forgets you, I can make a world that remembers only you.” Their arc is from possession to grief, accountability, and finally choice.

### Kite / 纸鸢 — Catdex keeper (Sunlit Garden)
A meticulous archivist who distrusts perfect records. Kite remembers the player as a person who always left the last page blank for someone else to write. Secret: Kite hid one torn page because it recorded the player's final argument with Muling; Kite feared the truth would make the player abandon the search.

### Feizhi / 绯枝 — Shrine keeper (Sakura Woods)
Warm, funny, and apparently superstitious. She treats fortunes as questions, not predictions. Secret: she has been rewriting the shrine's old prophecy because the original wording was used to justify the Star Bell experiment. Her arc is learning that preserving a tradition can mean admitting it was wrong.

### Duchuan / 渡川 — Ferryman (Creek Valley)
Dry humour, patient listener, never answers a question before asking what the player is afraid the answer will be. Secret: the river did not steal the player's name; the player placed it in the current so Muling could not be used as the seal's anchor. His arc is moving from detached witness to someone willing to testify.

### Jinhe / 烬禾 — Ember warden (Volcano)
Blunt, practical, distrusts sentimentality. She survived the catastrophe and has spent years maintaining the seal. Secret: she saw the player turn back toward the Bell when there was still a way out. She did not intervene because the player asked her to protect the others. Her arc is accepting that keeping a promise in silence can become another kind of abandonment.

### Star Scribe / 星簿 — Archivist of Moonshadow Ruins
Soft-spoken, precise, and morally compromised. He has studied the Bell long enough to know Muling's ritual cannot restore the player. He withheld this because he hoped the player would stop Muling before the ritual reached its final phase. His arc is confessing that knowledge without courage is complicity.

## 4. Five-chapter campaign

### Prologue — The bell rings backward
**Playable opening:** Begin on a cinematic close-up of a small bell cord caught on a branch. Wind moves the leaves; a paw enters frame and touches the cord. The sound arrives backwards. Cut to the player's chosen starter waking beneath the old garden bench. No long tutorial lecture.

**Dialogue beats:**
- Kite: “You woke up when the bell rang. That is not the same as being called.”
- Player: “Then who was calling?”
- Kite: “Someone who remembers you differently from the way you remember yourself.”

**Player objective:** choose a starter, meet Kite, learn the first exploration controls in context, then enter Sunlit Garden. The yard remains accessible as an optional sanctuary, not the main dashboard.

**Foreshadowing:** a second shadow briefly appears beside the player's shadow in the bell's reflection. It vanishes when the player turns.

### Chapter I — The Blank Page (Sunlit Garden)
**External story:** cats in the garden have forgotten familiar routes and names. A kitten keeps returning to the same empty patch of grass because it remembers waiting there for someone.

**Character turn:** Kite admits the Catdex has not lost pages by accident; someone has carefully removed the names, leaving the paper intact.

**Memory fragment:** two kittens make a promise at the garden gate: “If the bell rings, come home. Even if you are late.”

**Gameplay:** explore the garden, befriend the first wild cat, discover the yarn box, learn that environmental discoveries reveal memories and unlock routes. The first battle is framed as calming a frightened Hollow-touched creature, not defeating a disposable enemy.

**End beat:** the bell rings once from a place with no bell tower. The player's starter reacts as if it recognizes the sound.

### Chapter II — A Promise Beneath the Petals (Sakura Woods)
**External story:** the shrine's fortunes are changing overnight. Villagers receive fortunes written in the handwriting of cats they have forgotten.

**Character turn:** Feizhi reveals the old prophecy was edited. The original does not say “one bell, one wish”; it says “one bell, one willing name.” The distinction changes the meaning of the ritual.

**Memory fragment:** the player gives the bell cord to Muling. Muling jokes that the player will lose it before sunset. The player replies: “Then you will have to remember where I put it.”

**Gameplay:** follow petal trails, solve a paired-cat traversal puzzle, and use a duo-bond moment to cross a broken shrine bridge. The duo scene must be an authored illustration with subtle parallax/light/petal motion; do not substitute generic sliding cat sprites.

**End beat:** a fortune appears in fresh ink: “The one who waits will mistake silence for a promise.”

### Chapter III — The Name the River Carried (Creek Valley)
**External story:** the river returns objects but not their owners. Cats recognize keepsakes yet cannot remember who gave them.

**Character turn:** Duchuan tells the player the truth: the name was surrendered willingly. The player chose to make the Bell unable to use Muling as the anchor for the seal.

**Memory fragment:** Muling asks the player to promise they will never disappear. The player cannot make that promise. Instead, they promise to come back when the bell rings. The player knows even then that the promise may be impossible.

**Gameplay:** river-current navigation, paired-cat coordination, a rescue encounter where the player must choose which memory to stabilize first. The choice changes later dialogue and which NPC sends help in the volcano chapter, but does not lock the main ending.

**End beat:** the river reflects the player and Muling standing together; in the real world, only the player is there.

### Chapter IV — The Last Bellkeeper (Volcano)
**External story:** heat is leaking from the sealed Bell. The region's cats are losing small, ordinary memories first: favourite food, familiar songs, the route home. These details make the stakes intimate.

**Character turn:** Jinhe testifies that the player stayed behind voluntarily. She also admits she obeyed the player's request not to tell Muling, believing silence would spare them. The lie protected no one.

**Memory fragment:** the catastrophe begins. The player turns back toward the Bell. Muling reaches for them, but the closing seal cuts the sound in half. The player mouths “I'm coming back”; Muling hears nothing.

**Gameplay:** timed escape through shifting volcanic paths, rescue named cats, boss encounter against a Hollow guardian. The player sees that the stolen memories are being routed toward one silhouette inside the Bell.

**End beat / first major reversal:** the voice calling the player is not a trapped victim. It is the one who has been taking the memories.

### Chapter V — The Bell That Rings Backward (Moonshadow Ruins)
**External story:** the Star Scribe reveals the six melodies are not keys to open the Bell. They are the six emotional memories Muling needs to build a convincing echo of the player.

**Character turn:** Star Scribe confesses he knew the ritual could not restore the player. He kept silent because he thought the player would be safer not knowing. The player calls out the repeated pattern: everyone has made decisions “for” someone else and called it love.

**Memory fragment:** the final page is not a memory of the catastrophe. It is the moment after it: Muling, alone, holding the bell cord and deciding that if the world will not return the player, Muling will make the world give them back.

**Second reversal:** the player learns Muling did not start the catastrophe and did not cause the original disappearance. Muling did, however, knowingly choose to take memories once they understood the cost. Grief explains the choice; it does not absolve it.

**Gameplay:** a multi-room ruin dungeon. Each room revisits a visual motif from an earlier region, now with its meaning changed. The final chamber has a quiet conversation before combat. The player can ask three questions; each reveals a different part of Muling's motive. All paths lead to the confrontation, but the answers shape the final dialogue.

### Finale — The Last Page
**Boss encounter:** three phases, each with a narrative purpose:
1. **The Echo:** Muling fights using familiar duo moves from the player's recovered memories. The player recognizes the moves and understands they were once playful.
2. **The Borrowed Names:** Muling draws power from stolen memories. The player's companions break the links by calling the owners' names; the fight becomes about protecting the team, not maximizing damage.
3. **The Unfinished Promise:** Muling stops attacking and asks the player to stay. Combat pauses. The player answers with the truth: “I wanted to come back. I was not able to. And you were hurt. Both things are true.”

**Resolution:** the player refuses the ritual, but does not reject Muling. The six melodies are returned to their owners. Muling must face the people whose memories were taken and begin repairing the harm.

**Ending scene:** dawn at the garden gate. The bell rings once, normally. Muling asks, “If I call, will you come?” The player answers, “I will try. And if I cannot, it will not mean I stopped caring.” They sit together; the Catdex opens a blank first page. The player writes a new name—not the old one.

**Post-game:** Muling becomes a recruitable companion after a trust-building questline, not an instant reward. Revisit NPCs to see consequences, unlock paired-cat memory scenes, and uncover optional pages that deepen the world without undoing the ending.

## 5. Dialogue craft rules

- No NPC explains the entire world in one speech. Every scene should have a want, a resistance, and a change.
- Keep spoken lines short enough to feel performed. Let animation, pauses, and environmental sound carry meaning.
- Distinguish voices: Kite precise; Feizhi playful; Duchuan elliptical; Jinhe blunt; Star Scribe formal; Muling intimate and increasingly controlling.
- Avoid repeating “memory,” “bond,” “name,” and “promise” in every line. Use concrete sensory details: wet pawprints, a chipped bowl, a bell cord, a favourite path, a tune hummed off-key.
- Every chapter must contain one warm or funny beat. The story should not be relentlessly solemn.
- The player must ask questions and make observations. Do not make them a silent camera.
- The antagonist must be emotionally legible before the reveal, but the reveal must not be obvious from the first scene.
- Never frame Muling's memory theft as justified. Empathy and accountability must coexist.

## 6. Continuity checks

1. The Bell stores/replays/transfers memories; it does not resurrect the dead or rewrite time.
2. The player surrendered their name to protect Muling from becoming the seal's anchor.
3. Muling saw the disappearance, not the sacrifice; no one delivered the truth clearly afterward.
4. The six melodies help the player recover the truth and help Muling's ritual progress. This dual function is foreshadowed visually and in NPC dialogue.
5. NPCs have independent motives and consequences; they are not five interchangeable quest vendors.
6. The player does not simply forgive Muling because the boss is defeated.
7. The ending resolves the central question and leaves room for post-game play without nullifying the emotional closure.

## 7. Production requirements

- Cinematic prologue and ending use authored key art/illustration, layered depth, camera movement, lighting, particles and sound design. No paper-card slideshow as the primary presentation.
- Provide language toggle and skip controls on the prologue. Skip is always available after the opening beat; language switching must update an open scene immediately.
- NPCs appear physically in the world and can be approached. Use each character's painted character art, not placeholder line cats or generic enlarged sprites.
- Dialogue choices must be legible and keyboard/controller/touch accessible.
- Keep the garden as an optional sanctuary menu. The overworld map and story objectives are the primary RPG interface.
- Retain collection, raising, duo trust, duo memories, crafting and mini-games as optional support systems; they should enrich the campaign, not interrupt its pacing.
