## Release gates

1. First-time player can identify what to do next and why without opening the quest drawer or reading a guide. Check each scene boundary; do not prompt the participant. Record where they hesitate or ask for help.
2. Each required interaction must produce a new discovery, emotional beat or distinct mechanical challenge. Remove duplicate dial games, forced practice, rote melody repetition and gratuitous transitions; optional discoveries may be skipped. The actual browser test must prove main progression and never require optional practice.
3. Action-specific instructions precede action; outcome dialogue follows earned evidence. No unearned progression, no fabricated game evidence.
4. Portrait and landscape: primary action visible, hit-testable, 44 px targets, no overlap with actors or essential artwork; no placeholder sprites as final art.
5. Play a fresh uninterrupted Chinese game, followed by an English smoke. Record scene, elapsed active time, attempts, confusion, abandonment, repeated clicks, screenshot/video and actual revision. Independent human playtest judges whether story hooks and payoffs land; automated success alone is not approval.
6. Block final-quality signoff for unresolved confusion, forced low-value play, visually incoherent scenes or missing emotional beats. Report separately: code, deterministic test, browser evidence, human playability, deployment. Do not call the latter three complete when not observed.
