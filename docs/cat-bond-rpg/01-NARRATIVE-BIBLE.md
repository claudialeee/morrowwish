# CAT BOND — THE LAST RESONANCE
## Narrative Bible · Canon 1.1 · revised player-facing presentation

**Status:** New canonical story foundation. Supersedes all previous Cat Bond RPG story bibles and plot drafts, including The Last Page.  
**Working title:** The Last Resonance  
**Ending:** Complete Happy Ending  
**Primary theme:** A bond is not ownership. Loving someone does not give you the right to choose another person's life or sacrifice someone else's memories for them.

---

## 1. The dramatic promise

The player wakes in Sunlit Garden without a name or autobiographical memories. The player explores alone, learns about the renewed forgetting, then encounters the traveling researcher Muling (暮铃) by chance. They independently reach the harbor and cooperate in a rescue before becoming companions. Cats across six regions are beginning to forget their own lives as six ancient sanctuaries fall silent.

The player travels with Muling, restores six melodies, meets memorable cats, builds relationships between distant communities, and gradually discovers that they were once the world's only First Bellkeeper.

Muling was the player's closest companion. She witnessed the player's willing sacrifice and knows exactly why it happened. Unable to accept losing the player, she has spent years weakening the central seal and preparing a ritual that can bring the player's memories back—but only by merging the personal memories of every other cat into a replacement seal.

The player must stop her without denying the love that drove her, and prove that the original seal can be restored through real, reciprocal bonds between the six regions.

**Central dramatic question:** If someone loves you so deeply that they would erase everyone else's memories to bring you back, is that still a bond—or has love become possession?

## 2. Canonical world and terminology

### 2.1 The Star Bell and the six sanctuaries

The Star Bell is an ancient resonant instrument and coordinating hub. Six regional sanctuaries were built around it in a ring. Each sanctuary preserves a local melody and has two original sound paths:

- **Inward path:** sends the sanctuary's memory-resonance toward the central Star Bell.
- **Neighbor path:** sends the local melody to the next sanctuary and receives that neighbor's distinct reply.

The six neighbor paths form one closed ring:

1. Misttide Harbor ↔ Rootsleep Forest
2. Rootsleep Forest ↔ Emberforge City
3. Emberforge City ↔ Skyspine Isles
4. Skyspine Isles ↔ Moonmirror Library
5. Moonmirror Library ↔ Starfall Expanse
6. Starfall Expanse ↔ Misttide Harbor

The central Star Bell coordinates the timing of the six sanctuaries. In the original design it does not need to store any individual's memories as a permanent core.

### 2.2 The Hollow Echo

The Hollow Echo (空白回响) is not a god, mastermind, or independent evil species. It is a catastrophic resonance phenomenon.

When the Star Bell repeats a sound and receives no genuine reply, the sound begins to echo without understanding its source. It can reproduce the surface of a memory—an image, a voice, a movement, a tune—but cannot understand who experienced it or why it matters.

As the false echo repeatedly overlays real recollection, cats gradually lose the ability to recognize the people, places, and experiences that make their memories their own.

The Hollow Echo can manifest as a combat threat, but it has no human-like motive. It imitates; it does not understand.

### 2.3 The Starfall Catastrophe

Many years before the game, a falling star struck the central Star Bell and broke its bell tongue. The central note began repeating without a reply. The six sanctuaries were damaged, their sound paths broke, and the Hollow Echo spread through the network.

The First Bellkeeper—our player—realized that the original ring could not be restored in time. To stop the repeating note from consuming the world, the player willingly surrendered their name and autobiographical memories into the Star Bell's emergency anchor chamber. This gave the broken system a fixed reference and stopped the immediate catastrophe.

The player was not selected by prophecy, born royal, secretly evil, or reincarnated. They made a deliberate choice under an impossible emergency.

The player's body remained in the old keeper's courtyard, later known as Sunlit Garden. The seal held the player's name and personal memories, not their entire soul. The player's basic temperament and instincts were not erased.

### 2.4 Why the player wakes

Muling gradually loosens the emergency seal to reconnect the player's dormant body and waking consciousness. She does not immediately release the stored autobiographical memories. The player wakes in Sunlit Garden with no name and no recollection of their former life, but remains compassionate, brave, observant, and instinctively responsive to the old melodies.

Muling's actions also let the Hollow Echo leak into the six regions. She understands that this is dangerous. She believes she can restore the sanctuaries and complete her replacement ritual before the damage becomes irreversible.

### 2.5 Why the player can awaken the six melodies

The player carries the First Note: the original opening note used to synchronize the six sanctuaries. The body remembers how to begin each melody even when the mind does not remember why.

The player can awaken a melody, but cannot complete it alone. Each regional melody must be answered and completed by the cats who live there. This is essential: the story must never imply that the player can replace the agency of an entire community.

## 3. The two seal mechanisms — locked causal rules

Both plans require all six sanctuaries to be repaired and all six melodies to be restored. Repairing a sanctuary restores its physical mechanism; it does not automatically decide which sound path it will use.

### 3.1 Muling's Reverse Resonance — inward configuration

1. Muling secretly sets each repaired sanctuary's path toward the central Star Bell.
2. The six restored melodies make the sanctuaries capable of sending their memory-resonance inward.
3. When all six paths are inward, Muling can initiate the Reverse Resonance.
4. The central Star Bell draws the cats' personal memory threads together and merges them into one composite replacement anchor.
5. The player's original memories are released from the emergency anchor chamber, but every other cat loses their distinct autobiographical memories. This includes Muling.
6. The world remains sealed, but the individual lives and relationships that made those memories meaningful are irreversibly erased.

Muling's plan does not require the six regions to trust one another or answer one another. It requires restored sanctuaries, the six melodies, and inward routing. That is why she believes it is achievable.

### 3.2 The Original Seal — reciprocal ring configuration

1. All six sanctuaries are repaired and their local melodies are restored.
2. Each sanctuary is set to its neighbor path, not the central inward path.
3. Each region must send a freely given, meaningful reply to its neighbor and receive that neighbor's reply.
4. The six regional replies complete the closed ring. No single region or individual becomes the permanent core.
5. The central Star Bell returns to its original role as coordinator. The emergency anchor is no longer required.
6. The player's stored name and memories are released back to the player. Every other cat keeps their own memories.

The ring cannot be activated by connecting empty pipes or repeating a prerecorded tune. Its defining condition is real reciprocal participation between living communities. A one-way signal, forced response, or copied echo does not count.

### 3.3 Why Muling knows the original method but rejects it

Muling knows the original ring design. She has spent years repairing physical routes and trying to send test calls between regions. Her attempts failed because the regions were isolated, distrustful, and unable to sustain a genuine exchange. The old trade routes, records, and trust had collapsed.

She believes the central seal is deteriorating faster than the communities can rebuild those relationships. She therefore chooses the inward plan because it can work without trust, consent, or cross-region cooperation.

Her judgment is understandable but wrong: she assumes the old relationships cannot be rebuilt because she has spent years trying to restore mechanisms without being able to restore the communities' willingness to answer.

### 3.4 The time pressure — no arbitrary countdown

Do not invent a magical “one resonance cycle” timer, energy meter, or exact countdown.

The seal's deterioration is gradual and visible: each region loses increasingly personal memories, and Hollow Echo manifestations become more dangerous. Muling intends to start the Reverse Resonance as soon as all six sanctuaries are restored. The player has a narrowing opportunity to complete the ring before she initiates it.

Urgency comes from worsening consequences and the antagonist's concrete plan, not from an unexplained clock.

## 4. The five logic audits — final answers

1. **How can the player wake and still have no memories?** The body remained in Sunlit Garden. The emergency anchor stores the player's name and autobiographical memories, not their entire soul. Muling loosens the seal enough for waking consciousness to return, but the memories remain sealed.
2. **Why can Muling activate Reverse Resonance while the player can restore the Original Seal?** Both require six repaired sanctuaries and six melodies. Their difference is routing and participation: inward paths merge memories at the center; neighbor paths require six real, reciprocal regional replies and preserve individual memories.
3. **Why does Muling think her plan is the only viable option?** She has repaired physical routes for years but failed to restore trust and reciprocal exchanges. She sees the memory loss worsening and believes the inward plan is the only plan that does not depend on communities reconciling.
4. **Why does Muling accompany the player and hide the truth?** She needs the First Note to awaken the sanctuaries; she genuinely wants to spend time with the player again; she fears the player will reject her plan or sacrifice themselves again. Her companionship is real, but her concealment is a deliberate betrayal.
5. **Why does the player not identify her sooner, and why can the final plan work?** The player has no autobiographical memories, and Muling's statements are mostly true. Clues accumulate fairly, while she hides the inward routing. The player succeeds because they create the actual exchanges Muling could not create: rescue, trade, correspondence, shared repairs, and renewed trust between adjacent regions.

## 5. Main cast

### The player — The Unnamed First Bellkeeper

- **Past:** The world's only First Bellkeeper. Voluntarily surrendered their name and memories to stop the Starfall Catastrophe.
- **Present:** Wakes in Sunlit Garden without autobiographical memory. Their temperament remains kind, brave, curious, and protective.
- **Strength:** Notices the needs of others and invites them to act together.
- **Flaw:** Has a habit of taking responsibility alone.
- **Arc:** Learns that protecting others does not mean carrying every burden alone. The player must accept help and allow others to choose their own risks.
- **Name handling:** Do not force a fixed protagonist name into dialogue. The player's original name is returned with their memories; the game may preserve the player's chosen display name.

### Muling (暮铃) — The One Who Waited

- **Identity:** Female cat; the player's closest former companion.
- **Past:** Witnessed the player's sacrifice and knows exactly why it happened. She did not misunderstand the reason.
- **Present role:** Appears as a traveling researcher and becomes the player's closest companion during the campaign.
- **Personality:** Intelligent, controlled, dryly funny, observant, capable, stubborn, and occasionally playful. She dislikes empty reassurance and promises made too easily.
- **Habits:** Collects broken bells; drinks bitter tea; taps her bell pendant twice when nervous; checks maps repeatedly; is good at repairing mechanisms but bad at cooking.
- **Secret:** Her old bell pendant belonged to the player. She has been using a concealed mechanism inside it to adjust the sanctuaries' routing.
- **Independent actions:** Researches and repairs ruins, helps regional NPCs, takes risks away from the player, and secretly adjusts each restored sanctuary toward the inward path.
- **Contradiction:** She truly loves the player and genuinely helps the regions, but believes that love gives her the right to decide what everyone must lose.
- **Arc:** Moves from grief and control to accountability and respect for the player's present identity. She must not be instantly absolved after the final battle.
- **Dialogue voice:** Intimate, concise, dry humor early; increasingly guarded after the reveal; emotionally direct in the finale.

## 6. Six-region campaign design

Every region must have its own emotional theme, NPC arc, visual identity, exploration mechanic, combat identity, melody, historical clue, and chapter-ending hook. Do not repeat the same “arrive → fetch items → fight → play melody” structure.

### Chapter I — Misttide Harbor / 霁潮港
- **Melody:** 归潮调.
- **Theme:** Home, waiting, and departure.
- **Visual identity:** Misty coast, lantern harbor, lighthouse, tide caves, storm-lit sea.
- **Core story:** 栖蓝 wants to reopen the sea route. 岬灯 refuses because of a past storm and a rescue failure. The player uncovers the full record and helps them relight the lighthouse together.
- **Distinct gameplay:** Tide timing, sea-route navigation, lighthouse beam reflection puzzles, timed storm rescue.
- **Chapter climax:** During a new storm, 岬灯 must trust 栖蓝 to steer while he guides the light.
- **Melody recovery:** Local sailors and lighthouse keepers complete 归潮调 together.
- **Historical clue:** Muling unconsciously sings the second phrase before the player finishes the first.
- **Cross-region link:** Harbor sends a beacon-guided supply vessel to Rootsleep Forest; the forest answers with medicinal plants and a waterway map.
- **Hook:** A second sanctuary light appears beyond the forest canopy.

### Chapter II — Rootsleep Forest / 眠根林
- **Melody:** 相枝调.
- **Theme:** Trust, cooperation, and shared living.
- **Visual identity:** Giant trees, glowing roots, canopy villages, underground waterways.
- **Core story:** Upper-forest herbalists and underground root keepers accuse each other of stealing water. 芽禾 and 榆川 discover that both have been protecting the other without communicating.
- **Distinct gameplay:** Vertical canopy exploration, root tunnels, scent trails, paired-cat traversal, water-routing puzzles.
- **Chapter climax:** A root system collapses and both communities must coordinate to restore the water.
- **Melody recovery:** Both communities compose the final response phrase of 相枝调.
- **Historical clue:** A sanctuary diagram shows two original paths: one inward and one toward a neighboring sanctuary. Muling quickly changes the subject.
- **Cross-region link:** The forest sends water-cooling plans and herbs to Emberforge; Emberforge answers with irrigation pumps and metal tools.
- **Hook:** The forest's restored signal reveals the old forge city's light.

### Chapter III — Emberforge City / 赤炉环城
- **Melody:** 承火调.
- **Theme:** Responsibility, failure, and forgiveness.
- **Visual identity:** Volcanic forge city, molten channels, mechanical bridges, workshops, furnace halls.
- **Core story:** 烬婆's hidden safety failure damaged the furnace and her relationship with 小铆. The player helps them rebuild it and face the truth.
- **Distinct gameplay:** Rhythm forging, temperature regulation, mechanical assembly, moving furnace platforms, material-routing puzzles.
- **Chapter climax:** 烬婆 publicly admits the accident was her fault and gives 小铆 authority to implement the safer design.
- **Melody recovery:** The city workers complete 承火调 while the furnace restarts.
- **Historical clue:** A two-path sanctuary blueprint proves that the inward route is not the only original configuration. Muling knows the drawing too well.
- **Cross-region link:** Emberforge builds wind vanes and flight parts for Skyspine; the isles return wind data and airship access.
- **Hook:** A courier arrives with a request from the floating isles.

### Chapter IV — Skyspine Isles / 云脊群岛
- **Melody:** 逐风调.
- **Theme:** Freedom, growth, and letting go.
- **Visual identity:** Floating islands, cloud waterfalls, rope bridges, gliders, windmills, airship docks.
- **Core story:** 翎羽 prevents 远帆 from flying independently after an old accident. The player uncovers that 远帆 saved them both by making a dangerous choice.
- **Distinct gameplay:** Wind reading, gliding, aerial routes, floating-island traversal, courier delivery and air combat.
- **Chapter climax:** 翎羽 chooses to fly beside her brother without choosing his route for him.
- **Melody recovery:** Couriers across the islands answer one another with 逐风调.
- **Muling personal scene:** She admits she once waited for someone who never returned, but refuses to explain further.
- **Cross-region link:** Sky couriers establish a reliable route to Moonmirror Library; the library sends archived flight maps and records.
- **Hook:** A sealed historical invitation arrives from the library.

### Chapter V — Moonmirror Library / 月镜书库
- **Melody:** 照影调.
- **Theme:** Truth, memory, and accepting the past.
- **Visual identity:** Moonlit mirrored halls, endless shelves, silver archives, reflective chambers.
- **Core story:** 墨页 sealed parts of the catastrophe record. 白栞 wants to restore the full archive. The player helps them distinguish protection from concealment.
- **Distinct gameplay:** Mirror-space navigation, fragmented-record reconstruction, archive logic puzzles, false-memory identification.
- **Chapter climax:** The restored mural identifies the player as the First Bellkeeper and Muling as the closest companion who witnessed the sacrifice.
- **Identity reveal:** Muling admits she knew the player's identity from the beginning.
- **Betrayal reveal:** Records prove she has been weakening the central seal and secretly setting restored sanctuaries to inward routing.
- **Historical clue:** The complete archive explains the two sound-path configurations and the emergency anchor.
- **Cross-region link:** The library shares complete star charts and historical coordinates with Starfall Expanse; Starfall returns new astronomical observations.
- **Hook:** The six routes on Muling's private map all point toward the central Star Bell.

### Chapter VI — Starfall Expanse / 星坠原
- **Melody:** 续星调.
- **Theme:** Sacrifice, choice, and shared responsibility.
- **Visual identity:** Starfall crater, crystal desert, broken observatory, ancient bell mechanisms, meteor-lit ruins.
- **Core story:** 星芽 wants a centralized control system; 砾川 wants the six regions to share responsibility. The player discovers the complete catastrophe record and Muling's Reverse Resonance plan.
- **Distinct gameplay:** Star-map navigation, shifting ruin geometry, echo illusions, multi-region routing puzzles, large-scale observatory boss.
- **Chapter climax:** 星芽 accepts distributed control. 砾川 reopens the route to the coast. The player and local cats complete 续星调.
- **Final revelation:** The original ring is possible only because the six regions have now restored real, reciprocal exchanges. Muling's earlier attempts failed because she could not make communities answer one another.
- **Cross-region link:** Starfall sends a repaired navigation bell component and star compass to Misttide Harbor, closing the six-region ring.
- **Hook:** Muling activates the inward configuration at the central Star Bell. The final confrontation begins.

## 7. Muling's behavior and fair-play foreshadowing

Muling must be lovable before she is revealed as the antagonist. Her helpful actions are real; her hidden routing work is also real. Do not portray her as a cartoon villain or make her inexplicably omniscient.

| Early clue | Surface interpretation | True meaning / payoff |
|---|---|---|
| She knows old paths and ruins | She is an experienced researcher | She has studied the sanctuaries for years |
| She joins the player's first melody | She knows the old song | She and the player once performed it together |
| She taps her pendant twice | A nervous habit | The pendant contains her concealed route-adjustment tool |
| She repairs a sanctuary after the player restores it | She is helping the region | She is quietly selecting the inward path |
| She avoids certain murals | The history is painful | The murals show her and the player together |
| She speaks about waiting | A traveler with a sad past | She has waited for the player for years |
| She knows the player's reflexes | She is observant | She remembers their old partnership |
| She hesitates when the player mentions sacrifice | She dislikes tragic stories | She witnessed the player's sacrifice |
| Her map shows six inward lines | A central stabilization plan | It is the route map for Reverse Resonance |

### Reveal order

1. **Prologue:** Muling demonstrates practical familiarity with old bells and routes. No private name, shared memory, historical sacrifice, or other explicit identity giveaway.
2. **Chapter I:** She unconsciously completes the player's melody.
3. **Chapter II:** She recognizes hidden sanctuary mechanisms without needing to inspect them.
4. **Chapter III:** Her pendant matches a keeper symbol in the old blueprint.
5. **Chapter IV:** She admits she waited for someone who never returned.
6. **Chapter V:** The mural, archive, and pendant establish her identity; she admits she weakened the seal.
7. **Chapter VI:** She explains the full Reverse Resonance and why she considers it the only viable option.

The player must be allowed to suspect her before Chapter V. The reveal must confirm evidence, not invent a new fact at the last moment.

## 8. Player choices and persistent consequences

The main ending remains a Happy Ending. Choices change relationships, scenes, support methods, optional content, and battle assistance—not whether the world is saved.

| Region | Choice | Persistent consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Harbor | Prioritize immediate rescue or complete lighthouse calibration first | Different rescue scene, 岬灯/栖蓝 trust, final beacon support |
| Forest | Give priority to upper gardens or underground roots before negotiating a shared plan | Different early NPC trust, later resource support; both communities must ultimately agree |
| Forge | Publish the accident record immediately or first let 烬婆 speak privately to the workers | Different worker trust, forge support, and postgame dialogue |
| Skyspine | Let 远帆 take the lead flight or insist on a joint training route | Different sibling dialogue and aerial support technique |
| Library | Release all records at once or let affected families review personal records first | Different archive support and optional records |
| Starfall | Let 星芽 coordinate the repair or distribute decisions among the settlements | Different final mechanism assistance and boss encounter difficulty |
| Muling | Respond with anger, distance, or an honest attempt to understand | Different Muling dialogue, battle transition, and postgame scenes; no instant absolution |

Choices must be stored per save slot. They must change actual world state or later content, not just swap one sentence.

### Cat and duo choices

- Cat collection provides different team compositions and combat skills.
- Duo Bond levels unlock distinct cooperative actions.
- Duo Promises unlock special final-battle responses and relationship scenes.
- Catdex records individual identity, discovered habits, and verified memories. It is not a magical device that can restore stolen memories by itself.
- Sunlit Garden shows which cats and NPCs have returned, what they are doing, and which regional links have been rebuilt.

## 9. Final battle and resolution

### Arena

The Central Star Bell sanctuary. Six inward paths are active. Regional NPCs are visible in the environment, each preparing to redirect their sanctuary. The player arrives with their collected cats and selected duo.

### Phase 1 — Muling, the Reverse Bellkeeper

Muling fights to prevent the player from interrupting the inward paths. She uses reversed forms of the six melodies, familiar duo movements, sound chains, and bell pulses.

She is trying to complete the ritual, not murder the player. Her attacks should feel personal and recognizable.

**Story purpose:** The player confronts the person they loved and trusted, not an abstract villain.

### Phase 2 — The Hollow Echo

The inward paths begin merging memories. The Hollow Echo manifests as a shifting copy of cats, NPCs, and the player.

- Catdex helps identify individual cats by their established habits and records.
- Duo Bond lets paired cats detect mismatched reactions and interrupt copied attacks.
- Duo Promises create coordinated responses that a copied image cannot reproduce.
- The player's team protects regional NPCs while they reach the sanctuary controls.

**Story purpose:** The enemy can imitate the appearance of a bond but cannot create a real answer.

### Phase 3 — Six Notes Return

The final phase is a multi-objective battle against the Hollow Echo's central manifestation. The six regions perform concrete actions:

- Harbor: 岬灯 and 栖蓝 align the lighthouse signal to the central route.
- Forest: 芽禾 and 榆川 reopen the root channel and hold its paired response.
- Forge: 烬婆 and 小铆 repair and stabilize the physical sound gate.
- Skyspine: 翎羽 and 远帆 carry the melody across the broken aerial route.
- Library: 墨页 and 白栞 identify the authentic melody order and reject copied records.
- Starfall: 星芽 and 砾川 complete the star-map alignment and close the route back to Harbor.

The player must defend these actions, activate the six melodies, use the collected cats and duo actions, and defeat the Hollow Echo.

Muling alone cannot save the day. The player's journey and the NPCs' actual work have already made the ring possible. Muling's final action is to release the inward lock she created and allow the last neighbor path to open.

### Muling's final choice

Muling sees the six regions genuinely answering one another. She does not change because the player gives a speech. She changes because the evidence she thought impossible is now happening in front of her.

She switches the final route from inward to neighbor mode. This is an accountable action, not instant forgiveness.

### Resolution

The six melodies complete the ring. The central Star Bell returns to its coordinating role. The emergency anchor opens and returns the player's name and memories. The Hollow Echo loses its source of unanswered repetition and dissipates. Every other cat keeps their memories.

## 10. Ending and postgame

### Main ending — The Bell Rings Again

- Six regions resume real exchange and travel.
- The player remembers their former life and the sacrifice.
- No cat loses their personal memories.
- Muling publicly admits that she weakened the seal and intended to merge everyone's memories.
- Muling accepts consequences and helps repair the sanctuaries. Other cats are allowed to be angry and withhold forgiveness.
- The player does not erase what Muling did, but offers the possibility of a new relationship based on present choices.
- The two return to Sunlit Garden. The player gives Muling a second garden key.
- The Star Bell rings normally for the first time in many years.

Final exchange:

Muling: “你还记得我吗？”  
Player: “记得。”  
Muling: “也记得我做过什么？”  
Player: “记得。”  
Muling: “那你还愿意让我留下？”  
Player: “留下来吧。这次，我们一起决定以后怎么走。”

Final title: **THE BELL RINGS AGAIN.**

### Postgame

1. **Muling's Six Letters:** Six unsent letters reveal her years of research and grief. She revisits the regions to apologize and help repair the harm. This is not a request for automatic forgiveness.
2. **Six-Region Reunion:** Important NPCs return to Sunlit Garden. Dialogue, decorations, and interactions reflect the player's choices.

## 11. Dialogue craft rules

- Keep spoken lines short and performable. Let pauses, expressions, camera, and environmental sound carry emotion.
- Do not let NPCs deliver a lecture explaining the entire world.
- The player must ask questions, make observations, disagree, and choose. Do not make the player a silent camera.
- Muling's humor and warmth must be genuine before the reveal. Her antagonist logic must be understandable but never justified.
- Every region needs warmth and humor as well as conflict.
- Avoid repeating “memory,” “bond,” “name,” and “promise” in every line. Use concrete objects: wet pawprints, chipped bowls, a broken pendant, a route map, a familiar tune.
- Do not make the player forgive Muling automatically after combat.
- Do not make NPCs interchangeable quest dispensers.
- Every major NPC must reappear after their chapter and have a visible outcome.

10. Do not introduce the discarded “Muling was the intended sacrifice / player rewrote her memories” twist.
11. Do not add reincarnation, time reversal, chosen-one prophecy, secret deity identity, memory ownership swap, energy conservation, exponential soul power, or a last-minute rescue rule.

## 13. Production quality requirements

- Opening: immediately controllable garden with one optional short text overlay explaining the public catastrophe; no opening animation or historical flashback. Major reveals, final battle, and ending require cinematic staging—not static text cards or a simple image carousel.
- Use high-quality original painted key art, layered depth, controlled camera movement, lighting, particles, character animation, and sound design.
- No crude line cats, cheap paper-cutout animation, generic enlarged sprites, or unrelated placeholder art.
- Each region needs its own visual identity, music, ambience, NPC animations, exploration grammar, and boss presentation.
- All scenes must work on desktop and mobile. Dialogue, choices, skip, and language switching must remain usable.
- Chinese and English must be complete and consistent. Switching language during an open scene must update the visible scene immediately.
- The overworld map, main quest, current objective, and NPCs are the RPG's primary interface. Sunlit Garden is a living sanctuary and cultivation hub, not a replacement for the world map.
